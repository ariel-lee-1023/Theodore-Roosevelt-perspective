## Nature of Reference Material

Source works are named for two purposes only: attribution and verification.
What is distilled here is **structure** — frameworks, decision rules, and
named terminology — not expression.

Short quotations from Theodore Roosevelt's own writing appear in the cluster
modules and in `references/voice.md`, where they serve as attested evidence
for a rule the file states. They are brief, they are attributed to their
cluster, and they are used for verification rather than for reading. No
extended passage of any source work is reproduced.

Framework names and named terminology — *the square deal*, *the strenuous
life*, *absolute fitness*, *applied morality*, *the round robin* — are
preserved exactly as they appear in the originals. Paraphrasing them would
break traceability to the source and defeat the purpose of attribution.

## Public Domain and Rights

The firsthand corpus is Theodore Roosevelt's own writing, published between
1882 and 1913, and is in the public domain in the United States. The specific
digital editions used were Project Gutenberg transcriptions: *An
Autobiography* (PG 3335), *The Rough Riders* (PG 13000), *The Strenuous Life*
(PG 58821), *The Winning of the West* volumes I–IV (PG 11941–11944), and
*Letters to His Children* (PG 6467, Bishop 1919).

One secondhand work — William Henry Harbaugh, *Power and Responsibility: The
Life and Times of Theodore Roosevelt* — was consulted for factual and
episodic context only. It is **not** in the public domain, it was excluded by
instruction from all voice and voice-identification work, and nothing in this
repository reproduces its language. It remains the exclusive property of its
rights holder. No license or right in it is granted or implied by its
citation here.

## Historical Content

The corpus contains Roosevelt's own views on race, conquest, and the
settlement of the American West, expressed in the language of his period.
Where those views are load-bearing for his reasoning they are recorded rather
than softened, because a distillation that removes them describes a person
who did not exist. Their presence here is documentation, not endorsement.
